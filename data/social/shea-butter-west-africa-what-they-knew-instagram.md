Women in West Africa applied shea butter to newborn skin centuries before the skincare industry existed. They were right.

Archaeologists have documented shea butter production in Burkina Faso since 100 CE. That is not a legend. That is 1,900 years before most Western beauty brands started putting it in their formulas.

Here is what those women already knew:

When you apply unrefined shea to skin, the triterpenes in it, compounds called lupeol and butyrospermol, reduce inflammation at the cellular level. They did not know the chemistry. They knew that wounds healed faster and that wind-burned skin recovered. They knew that shea kept infant skin protected from the dry season's heat.

Modern research has confirmed every part of this. Shea extract reduces the exact inflammatory markers your skin raises under UV, dry air, and environmental stress. It reduces transepidermal water loss by nearly 40 percent within 24 hours.

The knowledge was real. What most mass-market shea products sell you is a stripped-down version, refined at high heat, with the active compounds removed.

The women in Burkina Faso were not refining their shea.
