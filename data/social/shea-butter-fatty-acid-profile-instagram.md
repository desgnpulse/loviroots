Shea butter does not coat your skin the way other plant oils do. Its fatty acids are already in your skin barrier.

Your skin barrier is built from stearic acid, oleic acid, and linoleic acid. Shea butter contains all three, in proportions close to what your skin already produces. That is why it integrates with the barrier instead of sitting on top.

The stearic acid in shea is the same fat your stratum corneum uses as raw material. When the barrier is dry, cracked, or reactive, shea supplies something the skin can rebuild with.

Most other plant oils do not do this. Coconut oil is mostly lauric acid, which does not occur naturally in skin. It works as a short-term moisturiser. Shea works differently.

This is also why unrefined shea performs better on reactive skin. Refined shea keeps the fatty acids largely intact. But it loses the unsaponifiable fraction, the part that calms inflammation. You want both.

Check the colour when you buy. Ivory to pale yellow means unrefined. White means it has been bleached.
