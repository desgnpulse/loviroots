Your skin actually loses the most water around 8pm. Applying shea butter at night is not just a habit.

It is backed by your skin's daily rhythm. Scientists who measure water escaping through the skin surface find the rate peaks in the evening and is lowest in the morning. By the time you reach for moisturiser mid-afternoon, you have already missed the window your skin needed most.

Shea butter works on two levels. Its oleic acid penetrates and carries hydration in. Its stearic acid stays on the surface and slows water loss from escaping. Apply right after your evening shower, skin still slightly damp, and both do their best work. The dampness matters: it lets the oleic acid absorb rather than sit on top.

Between 10pm and 2am, skin is in active repair. Cell renewal peaks, collagen synthesis increases, the barrier rebuilds. Having shea butter in place before this window means the support is already there when the work starts.

Morning use still helps. A small amount before the day creates a light shield. But if you are choosing one time, choose night.
