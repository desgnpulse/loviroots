Cocoa butter and shea butter are not the same product. Here is what changes on your skin.

Both feel similar in your hand. Both call themselves natural moisturisers. But once they are on your skin, they do completely different things.

Shea butter contains oleic acid, a fat with a small molecular structure that passes through your skin's outer layer and reaches deeper into the epidermis. It also carries linoleic acid, a building block your skin uses to make ceramides. Ceramides are what keep your moisture barrier intact.

Cocoa butter does something different. Its dominant fats, stearic acid and palmitic acid, are heavier molecules that do not penetrate. They stay at the surface and form an occlusive film. That film is useful. It slows water evaporation and protects against dry air. But it does not go inside.

This is the real difference: cocoa butter seals your skin, shea butter feeds it.

If your skin stays dry no matter what you put on it, you may be reaching for the sealer when what you actually need is the feeder.
