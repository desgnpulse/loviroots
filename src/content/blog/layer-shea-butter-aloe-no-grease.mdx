Your shea butter is not greasy. You are applying it at the wrong time.

It is a description of what happens when an oil-based ingredient meets dry skin with nothing underneath it.

---

Shea butter is an occlusive. It creates a physical layer on the surface of your skin that slows water loss. It does not add hydration on its own. It holds hydration in. This is the job it was designed to do, and it does it well.

Aloe vera is a humectant. It does the other part. It draws water into the outer layer of your skin, the stratum corneum, and delivers it there. Aloe works quickly. You feel the effect within minutes.

These two ingredients address different parts of the same problem. One brings water in. The other keeps it there.

---

Most people apply them in the wrong order. Or they apply shea butter alone to skin that has nothing underneath it.

When you put shea butter on dry skin, it cannot do what it is meant to do. There is no water to seal in. Instead, the oleic acid in the shea butter, which absorbs slowly, sits on the surface while your skin waits. That slow absorption is what you feel as grease.

It is not a texture problem. It is a timing problem.

---

There is a second thing worth knowing about Nairobi's climate. In dry months, the humidity drops enough that a humectant used alone will pull moisture from deeper skin layers. Not from the air. Your skin ends up drier than before.

An occlusive on top prevents that from happening. It gives the humectant somewhere to keep the moisture it has drawn in.

This is not a niche skincare theory. It is why dermatologists describe the correct application order as: humectant first, then emollient, then occlusive.

Aloe vera, then shea butter. In that order.

---

Here is what this looks like in practice.

After your shower, while your skin is still slightly damp, apply aloe vera gel to your face and body. You do not need to wait for your skin to be completely dry. Damp skin is the point. The aloe has moisture to work with.

Wait 60 seconds. Sixty is enough.

Then take a pea-sized amount of shea butter. For your full face, that amount is sufficient. Press it into your skin with your palms. Do not rub it back and forth. Pressing distributes it evenly without dragging.

The shea butter is now sealing in the moisture the aloe has pulled in. That is it. That is the routine.

---

If you do this consistently, your skin holds moisture differently. The hydration is there in the morning, not just in the first twenty minutes after you applied the product.
