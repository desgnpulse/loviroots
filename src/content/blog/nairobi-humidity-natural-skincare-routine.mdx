Nairobi sits at 72% average humidity across the year. In July, it reaches about 79%. Most skincare content was written for cities at half that number.

That gap explains why routines with five or seven steps feel like they were designed for someone else's skin. They were. Humid air already does part of the moisturising work. Your routine should account for that instead of ignoring it.

---

Here is what is actually happening. Your skin pulls water in from humid air through passive absorption. Without something to hold it in place, that water escapes back out through the outer skin layers. Dermatologists call this transepidermal water loss. It is what turns a humid morning into dry, tight skin by noon, especially for anyone spending the day in air conditioning.

The fix is not more product. It is the right three steps, in the right order.

---

Step one: a humectant.

Aloe vera draws water toward the skin surface and holds it there. Its value in a daily routine is not the wound-healing reputation or the sunburn association. It is the weight and speed. Aloe absorbs in under a minute, leaves no film, and does not compete with anything you apply next.

Apply a thin layer immediately after washing, while your skin is still slightly damp. That residual moisture on your face is what the aloe works with. One layer. Give it 60 seconds before the next step.

---

Step two: an emollient.

Aloe draws moisture in but cannot hold it alone. An emollient fills the gaps between skin cells and gives the hydration somewhere to stay.

Shea butter is both an emollient and a light occlusive. In humid conditions, a pea-sized amount, warmed in your palms before applying, is enough for the face. Apply it over the aloe. Do not rush past the warming step. Cold shea butter applied directly to skin absorbs unevenly and leaves patches.

More product in humid air does not mean better hydration. It means product sitting on the surface, mixing with sweat and dust.

If you are using Lovi Shea Butter, the aloe is already blended in. One product handles both steps. Apply to damp skin for the same result.

---

Step three: protection.

In the morning, nothing applied before this step holds if ultraviolet exposure is undoing it daily. Nairobi gets consistent sun year-round. Cloud cover reduces it. It does not eliminate it. End your morning routine with sunscreen.

At night, replace sunscreen with a slightly more generous application of shea butter. Overnight is when your skin barrier does its repair work. Unrefined shea contains triterpenes and tocopherols, compounds found naturally in the karite tree, that actively support that repair process rather than just sealing the surface.

---

That is the full routine: cleanser, aloe vera, shea butter, sunscreen. Four products. Three functional steps. All of them sourced in or near East Africa.

Next time you read about a seven-step sequence, ask which climate it was designed for. Most were not designed for yours. Now you have one that was.
