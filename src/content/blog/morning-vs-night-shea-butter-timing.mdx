Around 8pm, your skin loses water faster than at any other point in the day.

This is not about the weather or how much you drank. It is built into your skin's biology. Scientists who measure transepidermal water loss, the rate at which water evaporates through your skin surface, find it follows a daily rhythm. It is lowest in the morning, around 8am. It peaks in the evening. The difference between the low point and the high point can be significant.

Most people reach for moisturiser when skin feels dry. That is usually mid-morning, or after a long day in an air-conditioned office. By then, the peak window has already come and gone.

---

Shea butter has two dominant fatty acids, and they behave very differently on your skin.

Oleic acid makes up about 46% of unrefined shea butter. Its molecular structure is relatively small. It penetrates through the skin barrier rather than sitting on top of it. It carries nutrients and hydration into the epidermis itself. When your skin is slightly damp and warm after a shower, absorption is faster.

Stearic acid makes up about 42%. It does not penetrate the same way. It stays closer to the surface and forms a physical layer that slows water from escaping. This is not a flaw. This is the seal.

Together, they make shea butter something most moisturisers are not: both a feed and a seal, working at two depths at once.

---

Morning application is not wrong. The stearic acid fraction still forms a protective layer before your day starts. In Nairobi, dust, pollution, and the cool dry air of the morning all meet skin that is already covered. That protection is real.

But morning use catches the low end of the daily cycle. Your skin is naturally least permeable in the early hours. You are sealing something that does not urgently need sealing yet.

At night, both fatty acids work together in the window when it actually matters.

Apply shea butter after your evening shower, while your skin is still warm and slightly damp. The dampness is not optional. It gives the oleic acid something to carry into the skin. Without it, both fatty acids sit closer to the surface rather than absorbing properly. The 10-minute window after washing is the best one.

Then, as your skin's water loss begins to climb through the evening, the stearic acid forms its seal. By the time your repair cycle peaks, between 10pm and 2am, when skin cell renewal is at its highest point and collagen synthesis increases, the barrier support is already in place.

You are not just moisturising. You are working with the window.

---

If your skin is very dry, use both. A smaller amount in the morning forms the day shield. A more generous amount at night does the deeper work. If you are choosing one, choose night.

Take two minutes after your shower, before you get dressed. Skin warm, slightly damp, not towelled dry. Press the shea butter in with your palms. Do not rub. Do not use more than you need. A small amount absorbed fully is more useful than a thick layer sitting on top.

The biology runs on a schedule. You are deciding whether to meet it.
